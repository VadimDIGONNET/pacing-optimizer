What is measured and what is assumed. Measured: the GPX track, the hourly weather, the published split times, start times and published rider masses. Assumed: CdA (0.21 m² for the reference rider, scaled by body surface area), Crr 0.0032, bike mass 8.5 kg, W' 25 kJ, cornering limit (calibrated on this stage). No rider power was measured: every watt in this report is inferred from times.

1. Course

GPX from cyclingstage.com, 26.075 km (-0.1 % versus the official 26.1 km), 472 m of climbing after smoothing (official: about 500 m). The Côte de Larringes measures +398 m over 9.54 km in the GPX (4.2 %), against +403 m and 4.4 % published. The true high point is at km 10.9 (807 m), 1.3 km after the categorised summit. Open-Meteo's 90 m terrain model agrees within a 3 m bias. 37 segments of 50 m have a centre-line radius under 40 m, most of them in the descent between km 14.5 and 17.

2. Weather

Open-Meteo archive at three points along the course, interpolated to each 50 m segment at each rider's passage time. Around 17:30 CEST: 25 °C, 33 % humidity, 976 hPa at the start, wind 4.4 m/s from the NNE at 10 m, i.e. 2.2 m/s at rider height (log profile, roughness 0.10 m). Air density 1.09 to 1.14 kg/m³ along the course. The climb has a slight tailwind, the lakeside finish a slight headwind. An independent observation (bikeraceinfo: 26 °C, NNE 11 km/h at the finish) agrees.

4. Validation: predicting a withheld checkpoint

For each intermediate checkpoint, the model is refitted on the other checkpoints (constant power over the merged sector) and predicts the withheld one. The error mixes the physics with the assumption of constant pacing across the merged sector, which is why the km 9.7 checkpoint (merging the second half of the climb with the descent) is the hardest. 5. Physiology check (GoldenCheetah OpenData)

15 amateur and trained athletes (CC0 data) give CP between 2.4 and 6.0 W/kg and W' between 6 and 36 kJ (median 17 kJ, 228 J/kg). On rides that contain a season-best 3 to 20 min effort, Skiba's W'bal reaches a median minimum of -31 % of W': the model overstates W' depletion, so a CP fitted with "W'bal = 0 at exhaustion" is conservative. The 25 kJ assumed for the pros sits at the upper end of the amateur range, consistent with the 36 kJ of the strongest athlete in the sample (6.0 W/kg).

6. Strategies: real, constant and optimal

For each rider: CP fitted so that their reconstructed effort empties W' exactly; "constant" is the highest constant power that keeps W'bal positive everywhere (the binding point is the summit, not the finish); "optimal" is the block plan (500 m blocks, sections cut at km 9.64, 10.9 and 17.0) that minimises time under the same constraint. Riders without intermediate splits only have a finish time, so their real strategy is unknown.

7. Limits

  • Intermediate splits are available for the top 5 at each checkpoint only. ProCyclingStats and the official site hold the full set but forbid automated access; the pipeline accepts a manual export.
  • CdA, Crr, bike mass and W' are assumptions. The implied CP values (6.4 to 7.0 W/kg) are high, which suggests the reference CdA of 0.21 is slightly optimistic or the descent costs are still underestimated.
  • The GPX is a reconstruction by cyclingstage.com, not a race-day recording: corner radii in the descent are approximate, hence the calibrated rather than physical cornering limit.
  • Weather is hourly model data on a 1 to 10 km grid; gusts and local shelter along the lake are not resolved.
  • One stage so far. The multi-stage validation (phase 7) is pending the choice of courses.
